What's next for NISAR
Food & water

Crops and soil moisture

With a pass every 12 days, NISAR can follow crops from planting to harvest and see how wet the soil is – useful for farmers and drought warnings.

Disasters

Fast response

Radar works at night and through clouds and smoke, so maps of floods, fires and earthquake damage can be made when they are needed most.

Ice & infrastructure

Millimeter watch

Stacking many passes can reveal glaciers flowing, sea ice drifting, and cities, coasts, dams and bridges slowly sinking – before it becomes dangerous.

04 · Data & methods

All images come from the NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar (NISAR) mission, L-band, downloaded from the Alaska Satellite Facility (ASF DAAC). Burn scars and floods use the Level-2 GCOV product (radar backscatter, from the official latitude/longitude browse images, cropped with each scene's footprint coordinates). Ground motion uses the Level-2 GUNW product (unwrapped interferogram), converted from phase to centimeters with my own Python script (movement = phase × wavelength ÷ 4π, wavelength ≈ 23.8 cm), with low-coherence pixels hidden and a smooth atmospheric trend removed.
